# Two opposing robots make front-to-front contact while approaching the ball. Neither is clearly using more force or speed than the other. What should the referee generally call?
- [ ] Pushing by both robots, because they are both at fault.
- [ ] Pushing by the robot that touched the ball last, as a conventional criterion to penalize one robot.
- [ ] Always decide on one more forceful robot and penalize that, for consistency.
- [x] No pushing, because the forcefulness is balanced.
## Analysis
This case is explicitly mentioned in the rules. Fights for the ball are an important feature of soccer, and this exception to the pushing rule facilitates them.

# A stationary robot is performing a kick motion. An opponent falls after contact with the kicking robot. What is the best ruling?
- [ ] The stationary kicking robot can never be penalized for pushing under these conditions.
- [ ] The kicking robot must be penalized for pushing as usual.
- [ ] The opponent must be assigned a pushing penalty for obstruction.
- [x] It depends on whether the kick could have successfully hit the ball if left unobstructed.
## Analysis
Another explicit exception to the pushing rules. Obviously you can't kick robots for no reason, but if you were feasibly trying to kick the ball you won't be penalized if the situation changes once you can no longer abort the kick.

# Blue is awarded a kick-in. Blue kicks the ball directly into Red’s goal without any other player touching it. What is the correct ruling?
- [ ] Goal for Blue.
- [x] Goal kick for Red.
- [ ] Corner kick for Blue.
- [ ] Dropped ball.
## Analysis
Kick-in is an indirect free kick, so this is a textbook example of an invalid goal due to not being indirect.

# During a brief stop, a fallen robot is getting up. To what extent is this allowed?
- [ ] Always, so the game restarts with the least delay.
- [ ] Never, as robots must cease all motion.
- [ ] Only if the robot is not near the ball, so as not to disrupt it.
- [x] Only while finishing the current attempt, in the interest of stopping safely.
## Analysis
The rule for the brief stop is that a robot must come to a stop *safely* and as soon as possible. The middle of a get-up motion is not considered a safe position, but both standing still and lying down are. So you are allowed to continue an attempt that was already underway as the brief stop was called, but you must stop no matter whether it succeeds or fails.

# During a drop ball, which statement is correct?
- [ ] The team that last had possession of the ball gets to kick off.
- [ ] At most one robot from each team may be in the center circle.
- [x] A goal can be scored directly from a dropped ball.
- [ ] The ball is not in play until 10 seconds after the whistle.
## Analysis
Simple textbook question. No team gets special kickoff rights, no robot can be in the center circle, and the ball is in play immediately after the whistle.

# A robot is wandering aimlessly close to the edge of the field, completely alone. The team captain, suspecting technical issues, requests for pick-up. What should the referee do?
- [ ] Allow the pick-up because there is no tactical advantage to be gained in the game from this.
- [ ] Allow the pick-up if the opposing captain agrees.
- [x] Deny the pick-up unless the robot is in a dangerous situation likely to cause physical injury.
- [ ] Always deny the pick-up: the team has to wait until the robot gets penalized to perform maintenance.
## Analysis
This question exemplifies the general philosophy of request for pick-up. The first option was intended as a "trap" for former SPL teams, since that was the pjilosophy then. The second answer is a "resolution method" that is never acknowledged in the rules, and heavily discouraged (if not forbidden, even). The fourth answer echoes the current philosophy, but ignores the possibility of a dangerous situation.

# Blue kicks the ball and it deflects off a red robot before crossing the touchline. Who is awarded the kick-in?
- [x] Blue, because the last touch was red
- [ ] The team nearest the ball, as always
- [ ] Red, because blue kicked the ball last
- [ ] Dropped ball due to indeterminacy
## Analysis
Any touch counts for determining the free kick taker, even random rebounds. The second and fourth answers are concepts that just don't exist.

# A Booster K1 repeatedly walks into a fallen Booster T1 in the middle of its getup motion. The K1 is pushed away by the T1's arms and falls. What is the best ruling?
- [ ] It depends, but the K1 can never be penalized due to "pushing" a larger and heavier robot.
- [x] The K1 is penalized for pushing due to interference with the getup motion.
- [ ] The T1 is penalized for pushing because it made the K1 fall
- [ ] No robot can be penalized for pushing in this situation
## Analysis
This question exemplifies the "size doesn't count", "walking into a fallen robot is pushing", and "a fallen robot cannot push" principles.

# A defensive robot was already inside the avoidance zone when a free kick was called and is actively trying to leave. The kick is taken before it can and the ball rebounds on the robot. Will the robot be penalized? Will the free kick be retaken?
- [ ] No and no, because the robot was making an effort to move away
- [ ] The robot is penalized due to being inside the avoidance zone, but play continues normally.
- [x] The robot is not penalized because it was moving away, but the free kick is retaken due to interference, albeit involuntary.
- [ ] The robot is penalized for illegal position and the free kick is retaken.
## Analysis
The correct answer contains its own explanantion, really. This question's purpose was to reiterate what's supposed to happen in this particular case after saying it during the presentation. Few teams got this right during the quiz, so I'm glad I put it in.

# You walk by a field. Play has just restarted after a certain "Event X" that you missed. Red 3 (rightfully) takes the kick, then plays the ball again before anyone else. The referee calls an indirect free kick for Blue. What could "Event X" have been?
- [ ] Kick-off
- [x] Corner kick
- [ ] Local game stuck
- [ ] Insufficient information
## Analysis
This is kind of a weird question. The intended though process is that the indirect free kick was due to the "double touch rule", which means "Event X" must have been a free kick, as that is the only class of events subject to this rule. And corner kick is the one and only free kick among the answers.
